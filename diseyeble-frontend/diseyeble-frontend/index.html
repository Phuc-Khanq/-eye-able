02 / 05 · How it works

How eye tracking works with a normal webcam

No special hardware. In every webcam frame diseyeble tracks your eyes, head and nose. Your head steers the pointer like a mouse, your eyes click and confirm, and optional eye-pull and nose-assist modes help it travel. A long, deliberate blink clicks; ordinary blinks are ignored.

01

Find the face

Each webcam frame is scanned for a face and 478 landmark points, including the eyes, nose and mouth.

02

Measure eyes, head and nose

The iris centre is compared with the eye corners. The turn of your head and the tip of your nose are compared with your own resting face.

03

Calibrate in 2 seconds

When the camera first sees you, the pointer jumps to the middle of the screen: look at it for a moment. diseyeble learns where “straight ahead” is for your head and eyes. Say “calibrate” to do it again.

04

Point, blink and act

Your head steers the pointer: small turns aim finely, quick turns go far. Optional modes let your eyes pull it toward where you look, or your nose carry it to the screen edges. A filter smooths jitter and small wobbles, and diseyeble moves the real mouse. A long blink clicks exactly where the pointer is.

04 / 05 · Voice

Built to understand speech that others find hard to follow

Standard speech recognition struggles with slurred speech. On recordings of people with dysarthria (speech affected by cerebral palsy or ALS), the standard Whisper model got 41% of words wrong, and 71% for the most severely affected speaker. So we taught it to listen better.

  • Fine-tuned on dysarthric speech. During the hackathon we fine-tuned Whisper large-v3-turbo (LoRA) on the TORGO database: 5 speakers to train, 1 to choose settings, 2 kept apart for testing. Typical speech (LibriSpeech) was mixed in so it doesn't forget ordinary voices.
  • Errors cut by more than half. On the 2 test speakers it never heard: 41.4% → 19.4% of words wrong (801 recordings). Sentences 27.3% → 13.8%, single words 74.8% → 32.6%; mildly affected speaker 15.3% → 3.6%, severely affected 71.3% → 37.4%. Typical speech unchanged (1.98% → 2.01%), and still about 0.1 s for a 5-second clip.
  • Time to speak. The pause that ends a sentence is adjustable from 1 to 4 s, questions wait 12 s, and nothing is typed until you say yes or blink. Unsure words show in amber; a local model tidies the text, and “use original” brings back your exact words.
  • Forgiving commands. About 60 commands, each with several ways to say it. Filler words are dropped, near-misses still match, and known mishearings count only when an answer is expected.
  • Only your voice. Speech counts only when the camera saw your mouth moving as it was said, so other people talking don't trigger commands.
  • Your own voice, optionally. Record about 150 everyday phrases to adapt it further (experimental). It needs your consent, stays on your PC, and “delete my voice data” removes it.
  • English only for now. Vietnamese is a future goal.

A research result on 2 test speakers, not a clinical claim. TORGO speakers read the same prompts, so the sentence wording (not the voices) appeared in training; the check on brand-new sentences had only 5 sentences, too few to judge. TORGO's licence keeps the model on our PC, so the public download uses standard Whisper.
